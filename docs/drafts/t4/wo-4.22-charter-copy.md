## 1. The decision the order left me

**The description cannot carry the approved disclosure and all three places in
160 characters.** The order says to tell you rather than trim the disclosure
myself, so here is the arithmetic.

The approved footer wording is **352 characters**. The shortest form that keeps
both regulated facts, that Penn Jets is a broker and not a direct air carrier,
and that flights are flown by licensed direct air carriers, is 111 characters.
Leading with the geography costs 49.

| Option | Length | |
|---|---|---|
| A — three places, disclosure intact | **161** | **one character over** |
| B — Teterboro and Westchester only | **151** | fits |
| C — three places, "flights are operated by" | 164 | over |

**Option A, at 161 characters:**

> Charter from Teterboro, Westchester, Long Island. Penn Jets is an air charter
> broker, not a direct air carrier; flights operated by licensed direct air
> carriers.

**Option B, at 151 characters:**

> Charter from Teterboro and Westchester. Penn Jets is an air charter broker,
> not a direct air carrier; flights operated by licensed direct air carriers.

**I recommend A.** The 160 figure is not a hard limit; it is roughly where
Google stops displaying. One character over means the last word may be clipped
in a search result, and nothing else. Option B fits by dropping Long Island,
which is one of the three places you told me to lead with, and losing a market
to gain one character is the wrong trade.

**There is a third way if you prefer to be strictly under.** The description
could carry the geography alone and leave the disclosure to the page and the
footer, where it already appears in full. That reads better as a search result
and is legitimate, since the disclosure's job is to be on the page rather than
in the snippet. I did not put it in the table because it changes what the
order asked for rather than trading within it. Say the word if you want it.

carrier is the regulatory status. If you would rather the block used only the
